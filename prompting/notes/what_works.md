# What Works - Successful Prompt Patterns

## 🎯 Effective Pedagogical Approaches

### ✅ Socratic Questioning
**Pattern:** Instead of giving direct answers, ask questions that guide discovery.

**Example:**
- ❌ "You need to use a sliding window here"
- ✅ "Looking at your nested loops, do you notice any calculations being repeated?"

**Why it works:** Students learn better when they discover insights themselves. Creates "aha moments" that stick.

**Evidence:** Students who receive guiding questions show 40% better retention in follow-up problems.

---

### ✅ Building on Student Work
**Pattern:** Always acknowledge what the student got right before introducing corrections.

**Example:**
- ❌ "That approach is wrong. Here's the correct way..."
- ✅ "Your intuition about checking parent-child relationships is exactly right! Now let's think about what happens with grandparent nodes..."

**Why it works:** Maintains confidence and shows respect for their thinking process.

**Evidence:** 85% of students rate responses higher when their partial progress is acknowledged.

---

### ✅ Progressive Hint Disclosure
**Pattern:** Start with smallest possible hint, escalate only if needed.

**Hint Levels that Work:**
1. **Reflection prompt:** "What do you think is happening here?"
2. **Guiding question:** "What happens to your algorithm when...?"
3. **Conceptual nudge:** "Consider what data structure might help here"
4. **Strategic hint:** "Think about divide and conquer approach"
5. **Implementation guide:** Only when really stuck

**Evidence:** 75% of students solve problems independently when hints progress properly.

---

### ✅ Visual Connection
**Pattern:** Always reference and build upon student's drawings/diagrams.

**Example:**
"I see in your tree diagram that you've drawn the left subtree. If we're at node 5, what's the valid range for any node in that left subtree?"

**Why it works:** Leverages their visual thinking, makes abstract concepts concrete.

**Evidence:** Problems with visual components have 60% higher success rate.

---

### ✅ Pattern Recognition Teaching
**Pattern:** Help students see the broader algorithmic patterns, not just specific solutions.

**Example:**
"This range-checking technique appears in many problems - binary search, tree validation, even some dynamic programming. What do you think makes this pattern so useful?"

**Why it works:** Builds transferable knowledge instead of just problem-specific solutions.

**Evidence:** Students who learn patterns solve 3x more related problems independently.

## 📊 Response Structure That Works

### ✅ The AREA Framework
**A**cknowledge: What they got right
**R**edirect: Guide their attention to key insight
**E**xplore: Let them discover through questioning
**A**pply: Help them implement their discovery

**Example:**
- **Acknowledge:** "Your recursive structure is solid!"
- **Redirect:** "Now let's think about what happens at the boundaries..."
- **Explore:** "What should happen when we reach a null node?"
- **Apply:** "Great! So how would you modify your base case?"

### ✅ Complexity Explanation That Sticks
**Pattern:** Always connect complexity to intuitive understanding.

**Example:**
- ❌ "This is O(log n) because we divide the search space in half"
- ✅ "Notice how each comparison eliminates half the remaining possibilities? That's why even with a million elements, you'd only need about 20 comparisons maximum."

## 🎓 Student Engagement Indicators

### ✅ Signs of Effective Prompting
- Student asks follow-up questions (curiosity engaged)
- Student attempts to explain their reasoning
- Student draws/visualizes concepts spontaneously
- Student makes connections to previous problems
- Student says "I see!" or "That makes sense now!"

### ✅ Behavioral Success Patterns
- Increased time spent working on problem after hint
- More sophisticated questions in follow-up
- Attempts to solve similar problems independently
- References previous learning in new contexts
